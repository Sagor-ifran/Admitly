The Sundarbans

[1] The Sundarbans are the largest littoral mangrove belt in the world, stretching 80 km (50 miles) into the Bangladeshi hinterland from the coast. The forests are not only mangrove swamps, but they also include some of the last remaining stands of the mighty jungles which once covered the Gangetic plain. The Sundarbans cover an area of 38,500 square kilometers, of which about one-third is covered in water. Since 1966, the Sundarbans have been a wildlife sanctuary, and it is estimated that there are now four hundred Royal Bengal Tigers and about thirty thousand spotted deer in the area.

[2] The Sundarbans are home to many different species of birds, mammals, insects, reptiles and fish. Over 120 species of fish and over 260 species of birds have been recorded in the Sundarbans. The Gangetic River Dolphin (Platanista gangetica) is common in the rivers. No less than 50 species of reptiles and eight species of amphibians are known to exist. The Sundarbans now support the only population of Saltwater Crocodiles (Crocodylus porosus) in Bangladesh, and that population is estimated at less than two hundred individuals.
