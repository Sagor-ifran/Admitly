Sample essay

A rainy day in Bangladesh is unlike any other. When the monsoon arrives between June and September, the sky turns a deep grey, the air grows heavy with moisture, and the whole country seems to pause and listen to the rain.

In the morning, the first sound is the steady patter of raindrops on tin roofs. Narrow village paths turn to mud, paddy fields shine green and silver, and farmers wade through the water with their trousers rolled up. In the city, rickshaw pullers cover themselves with plastic sheets and push through flooded streets.

Inside the home, the day moves slowly. Children watch the rain from the window, hoping school will be cancelled, while the smell of khichuri and fried hilsa drifts from the kitchen. There is something comforting about eating a hot meal while thunder rumbles outside.

Yet the rain also brings difficulty. Roads flood, traffic stops, and many people are stuck on their way to work. Poor families in low-lying areas lose their belongings to rising water. The same rain that feeds the crops can also destroy a season's hard work.

To conclude, a rainy day in Bangladesh is a mixture of beauty and hardship. It cools the heat, gives life to the land, and brings families together, but it also reminds us how closely our lives depend on nature.
